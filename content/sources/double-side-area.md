Concept: why doubling the side of a square does not double its area. Area of a rectangle = length x width; a square has equal sides, so area = side x side. Area is measured in square units (how many unit squares cover the shape); perimeter is the length around it.

Scaling: if every side of a square is multiplied by k, the area is multiplied by k x k. Doubling the side gives 4 times the area (a 6 x 6 square splits into four 3 x 3 squares); tripling gives 9 times; the perimeter only doubles or triples.

Why: the side grows in two directions at once, length and width, so the number of unit squares grows in both directions. The same holds for rectangles when both length and width are multiplied by the same k. If only one side is doubled, the area only doubles.

Author's teaching data: 3 x 3 square = 9 square units; 6 x 6 = 36 = 4 x 9; 9 x 9 = 81 = 9 x 9. Paint, tiles, grass seed and pizza (flat, same thickness) scale with area, a fence or a frame scales with the perimeter.

Conditions to state: both sides scaled by the same factor; flat shapes; same thickness/coverage per square unit when talking about paint or tiles.

Safe actions: draw squares on grid paper, fold paper, lay out tiles or sticky notes. No measuring tools beyond a ruler.
