Concept: name the process that made the evidence, then the one test the conclusion could fail.

1. Where a rule can break (Wason). Four cards: triangle, circle, blue, orange; no other shapes or colours. Rule: a triangle has a blue back. Turn over the triangle and the orange card only; blue proves nothing. Change: orange becomes blue, then only the triangle. Transfer: rule "fragile parcels are reinforced", records fragile, not fragile, reinforced, not reinforced; open fragile and not reinforced.

2. Right answer, other channel (Pfungst, Clever Hans). Hans tapped answers by reading tiny movements of the questioner, who knew them. Change: a screen hides the questioner, the only person Hans could see; question still spoken. Transfer: a bot labels wolves on snow, dogs on grass; swap only the background of one picture. A changed label shows the background mattered; an unchanged label proves nothing.

3. Only survivors are visible (Wald). A damage map from returning aircraft lacks the damage that prevents a return; a lightly marked part may be vulnerable or rarely hit. Change: the log filter — a made-up log first shows only four returned drones with cargo-bay damage; all eight adds four lost drones with engine damage. Transfer: app reviews only from finishers, nine in ten five stars: this describes posted reviews, not everyone who started.

4. A flag is not a verdict: false alarms from the big non-target group can outnumber true hits. Model: 1 in 1,000 has a condition, all tested, every real case detected, 5 percent of the rest wrongly flagged: 1 real flag against about 50 false. Change: the same condition in 1 of 2, same group and test: about 95 percent real. Transfer: 10 of 1,000 archive files damaged; a checker flags all 10 plus one in ten of the rest: 10 damaged, 99 undamaged.

5. Three sources or one echo (New York Sun, 1835). Reprints without their own check add no observation; shared source means dependence, not falsehood. Transfer: three AI assistants cite one press release about a battery. No injury scenes.
