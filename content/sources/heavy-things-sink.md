Concept: density is mass per unit volume (density = mass / volume). Reference: 14.1 https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/14-1-fluids-density-and-pressure

Buoyant force (Archimedes' principle): a body in a fluid is pushed up by a force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. A fully submerged body displaces fluid of its own full volume.

Floating vs sinking for a solid uniform body released in still water: it sinks if its average density is greater than the density of water, and floats if its average density is less. Mass alone does not decide it; compare the body with water of the same volume. Water density for school problems: 1 g/cm3.

Cutting a uniform body in half halves both mass and volume, so density stays the same and the half behaves like the whole (a sinking stone's half still sinks).

Hollow bodies: what counts is average density of the whole body including the air inside (a steel ship floats because its hull with the air inside has a smaller average density than water; if water fills the hull, the average density rises and it sinks).

Author's teaching data (set by the author, not measurements): wooden block 600 g and 1000 cm3 -> 0.6 g/cm3, floats; stone 200 g and 100 cm3 -> 2 g/cm3, sinks. Transfer case: object A 400 g and 500 cm3 -> 0.8 g/cm3 floats; object B 150 g and 100 cm3 -> 1.5 g/cm3 sinks.

Conditions to state: body is uniform and has no hidden cavities unless the card is about hollow bodies; it does not soak up water or dissolve; it does not touch the bottom and nothing holds it; ordinary fresh water; surface tension is ignored for these large bodies.

Safe actions: thought experiments or everyday room-temperature objects in a bowl of water (plasticine ball vs plasticine boat, an apple, a closed empty bottle). No fire, no heating, no tasting.
