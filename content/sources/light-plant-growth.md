Concept: what light gives a plant. In photosynthesis a plant uses light energy to make sugar (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water; oxygen is released. Reference for sugar use: 8.3 https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-3-using-light-energy-to-make-organic-molecules

Matter vs energy: the material (mass) of new plant tissue comes mainly from carbon dioxide taken from the air and from water; light supplies the energy for the chemical reactions, not the material. "Light turns into wood" is a wrong model.

Carbon dioxide enters leaves through small openings (stomata). Water comes from the soil through the roots. Roots also take up minerals, which are needed in small amounts; most of the dry mass does not come from the soil.

Plants also respire all the time, day and night: they break down sugars to release energy for their cells. Sugars are both an energy source and building material for growth.

Without light a plant cannot make new sugar, but it does not stop instantly: it can live and even grow for a while using stored reserves (for example a potato sprouting in the dark). Long-term growth needs light.

Conditions to state: green plant, enough water, carbon dioxide in the air, suitable temperature; when comparing light vs no light, keep water, temperature and plant type the same.

Safe actions: only thought experiments or comparisons that do not require depriving a living plant of vital conditions in practice (observe sprouting potatoes, compare two pictures, imagine a sealed jar). No medical conclusions; do not claim plants in a bedroom are dangerous.
